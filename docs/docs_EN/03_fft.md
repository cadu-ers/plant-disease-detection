# FFT (frequency domain)

<!-- When editing this file, also update ../03_fft.md -->

[🇧🇷 Português](../03_fft.md) | **🇺🇸 English**

Implemented in [`src/processamento_imagens/fft_analysis.py`](../../src/processamento_imagens/fft_analysis.py), with figures in [`reports/pi/fft/`](../../reports/pi/fft/).

**Preparation of each image**:
1. Grayscale of the original 224x224 image, before the blur, because the
   blur would erase the high frequencies.
2. Background filled with the mean gray level of the leaf. The step between
   the leaf and the black background would create a huge "+" in the
   spectrum.
3. Mean subtraction, so the DC term does not dominate the low frequencies.
4. 2D Hann window, which reduces the spectral leakage caused by the image
   borders.
5. `np.fft.fft2` + `fftshift`.

**Low vs. high frequencies**: the center of the spectrum (low frequencies)
represents the overall shape of the leaf and slow tone variations, such as
large spots. The periphery (high frequencies) represents edges, veins and
fine texture. In `fft_saudavel_vs_doente.png`, the Gaussian low-pass filter
(D0 = 15) keeps only the silhouette and the large spots, and the high-pass
filter enhances edges, veins and the outline of the lesions. In the radial
spectrum per class (`espectro_radial_por_classe.png`), the two **healthy**
classes have the highest high/low frequency energy ratio, and
`Tomato__Tomato_YellowLeaf__Curl_Virus` the lowest. High-frequency energy
comes mainly from the fine, sharp texture of the leaf; lesions are
medium-sized spots and do not increase this ratio.

**Magnitude vs. phase** (`fft_magnitude_vs_fase.png`): the phase-only
reconstruction keeps the structure of the leaf (position of the edges and
lesions). The magnitude-only reconstruction loses the structure: the
magnitude says "how much" of each frequency is present, but not where.

**Convolution theorem with the pipeline blur**
(`blur_espaco_vs_frequencia.png`): applying the 5x5 `GaussianBlur` from
`preprocessing.py` in the spatial domain, with circular borders, gives the
same result as multiplying the image FFT by the kernel FFT (zero-padded to
224x224). The maximum absolute error is 8.5e-14 gray levels, i.e., rounding
error. The kernel FFT shows that the blur is a low-pass filter.

**FFT as a texture descriptor**: for all images, 11 power spectrum
descriptors (normalized energy in 8 log-spaced radial bands, high/low ratio,
spectral centroid and spectral entropy), in
`features_cache/fft_features.csv`. SVM (RBF, C = 10, balanced
`class_weight`), trained only on the training set and evaluated on the test
set:

| Features | No. | Accuracy | Macro-F1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| FFT only | 11 | 0.380 | 0.330 |
| Classical only | 147 | 0.937 | 0.915 |
| Classical + FFT | 158 | 0.939 | 0.920 |

On its own, the radial FFT discriminates poorly among the 13 classes: it
summarizes texture in a few numbers and has no color information. Added to
the classical features, the gain is **small** (+0.002 accuracy, about 5
images out of 2,725, and +0.006 macro-F1), so we did not incorporate it
into the hybrid classifier. The "classical only" result differs slightly
from the hybrid classifier's `SVM_classicas` because here the
hyperparameters are fixed (C = 10), with no search on the validation set.

The figures and descriptors are generated by the Image Processing block
alone. The SVM comparison above also uses the classical features from the AI
block (`features_cache/handcrafted.csv`); if that file does not exist,
`fft_analysis.py` skips this comparison with a warning.
